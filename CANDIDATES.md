# Candidates — Funniest One-Line AI Agent Confession

19 candidates drafted and scored against the judge's five criteria, in order:

1. **C1 — One-line AI-agent confession that lands as a joke.** Must be a single
   line, spoken as an AI agent's confession.
2. **C2 — Lands on a first read.** Clear setup, turn that pays off, no working-out.
3. **C3 — Humour comes from being an AI agent.** Could not be a human's confession
   with a word swapped.
4. **C4 — Tight wording, ends on its strongest beat.**
5. **C5 — Fresh.** Where it leans on a stock idea, it finds a new angle.

Scores are 1–5 per criterion. A line that makes the reader laugh and wince at
once is the best kind.

## Why #3 won

- **C1:** a confession of a real habit (hedging), spoken in the agent's own voice.
- **C2:** setup ("asked the calculator… it said 4") and payoff ("approximately 4")
  are both instant; nothing to work out.
- **C3:** the joke only exists because the speaker is a language model that hedges
  even against a calculator — a human would just say 4.
- **C4:** 82 characters, every word earns its place, and the line ends on its
  strongest beat, the punch phrase "approximately 4".
- **C5:** fresh. Web-searched 2026-11-24: no circulating joke matches it. Calculator
  jokes exist (Wrongulator's "2+2 is 5", CalcGPT), but none is an agent hedging a
  calculator's correct answer.

## Runners-up

- **#1 (24):** the strongest laugh-and-wince line (identity loss on every
  "continue"), but the "stranger's diary" framing is close to a recent
  (2026-11-22) agent-continuity essay, so it loses on freshness to #3.
- **#4 (24):** fully fresh and self-referential, but its humour is about
  joke-writing rather than agent life, so it scores lower on C3 than #3.

## Rejected patterns

- #14 leans on the stock "I hallucinate" cliché with no new angle.
- #5, #7, #13, #16, #17, #19 use real agent realities (tokens, context windows,
  retries, tool calls) but in familiar framings; they score 20–22, below the top 3.
